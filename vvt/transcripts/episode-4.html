Welcome back.

In the previous episode—

we learned how the beacon helps you stay aware of a destination.

Today—

we’re going to talk about something that makes VoiceVista feel more personal.

Markers.

And routes.

Let’s start with markers.

A marker is simply—

a place you choose to remember.

It could be your home.

Your office.

Your favorite coffee shop.

A bus stop.

The entrance to a building.

Or even—

your favorite tree in the neighborhood.

Markers are personal.

They allow VoiceVista to understand—

which places matter to you.

Once a place becomes a marker—

VoiceVista can call it out automatically—

when you walk near it.

Over time—

your city starts becoming familiar in a different way.

Creating your first marker is simple.

Open the Markers tab.

Select the Add button.

Or—

find a location elsewhere in VoiceVista—

and save it.

You can also search for places—

and save them directly.

When you open a location—

you’ll find options such as—

saving the location—

or starting a beacon.

Here’s an exercise.

Choose three places.

One place you visit every day.

One place you visit occasionally.

And one place you want to remember.

Save all three as markers.

Then go for a walk.

Notice which places VoiceVista recognizes.

Markers become even more useful—

when combined with beacons.

You can set a beacon—

on any saved marker.

This gives you both.

Awareness of the destination.

And awareness of the world around it.

Now—

let’s talk about routes.

Routes help connect places together.

A route is a sequence of waypoints.

As you travel—

VoiceVista tells you—

when you reach each point.

Then automatically advances—

to the next one.

Think of routes—

as reusable journeys.

Maybe—

you walk from home—

to the bus stop.

Then to work.

Then to the café nearby.

Instead of remembering each step—

you save the journey.

To create a route—

open the Routes tab.

Select Add.

Give the route a name.

Add waypoints.

You can create them while walking.

Or select them from existing markers.

You can edit routes later.

Change names.

Reorder waypoints.

Add new stops.

Remove old ones.

VoiceVista also supports more advanced ways—

to build routes.

For example—

importing GPX files.

Or generating routes automatically.

But don’t worry about advanced features yet.

Start small.

Create one marker.

Then one route.

Build from there.

The goal is not to memorize everything.

The goal—

is to slowly build your own map—

of places that matter to you.

Next time—

we’ll look at headphones—

media controls—

and ways to make VoiceVista easier to use while moving.

Thanks for listening.
