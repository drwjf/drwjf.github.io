Welcome back.

So far—

we’ve explored surroundings.

The beacon.

Markers.

And routes.

Today—

we’re going to make VoiceVista easier to use in everyday life.

This episode is about headphones.

Media controls.

And saving battery.

Let’s begin with headphones.

You do not need headphones to use VoiceVista.

But many users find that headphones make the experience more comfortable—

and more immersive.

VoiceVista supports wired headphones.

Bluetooth headphones.

And compatible AirPods.

When using compatible AirPods—

VoiceVista can use head tracking.

This means the app can understand—

which direction your head is facing.

That can make spatial audio feel more natural.

And it means—

you no longer need to actively hold the phone—

to keep directional information accurate.

If you want to connect supported headphones—

open Settings.

Go to General Settings.

Then open Head Tracking Headphones.

Before connecting—

make sure your headphones are already paired—

inside the iPhone Bluetooth settings.

Now—

let’s talk about media controls.

This is one of the most convenient features—

once it becomes part of your routine.

If your headphones support Apple’s media controls—

you can control VoiceVista—

without touching your phone.

Different headphones have different buttons.

But the most common actions are these.

Play—

or pause.

This silences current audio.

And if a beacon is active—

toggles the beacon sound.

Next.

Calls out—

What’s My Location.

Previous.

Repeats the last callout.

Skip Forward.

Turns automatic callouts—

on or off.

Skip Backward.

Triggers—

What’s Around Me.

Imagine walking with headphones.

Without unlocking your phone—

you repeat a callout.

Check your location.

Mute the beacon.

And continue walking.

Once learned—

this can become surprisingly natural.

Now—

let’s talk about battery.

VoiceVista uses location services.

Like any map app—

battery usage depends on how you use it.

One of the biggest factors—

is screen time.

If you are listening—

instead of interacting—

lock your screen whenever possible.

VoiceVista also includes Sleep Mode.

When enabled—

VoiceVista stops using location services—

and network activity—

until you wake it again.

There is also Snooze Mode.

This puts VoiceVista into a low power state—

until you leave your current location.

Think of Sleep—

as fully resting.

And Snooze—

as resting—

but staying ready.

One final tip.

Don’t feel like you must hear everything.

Mute the beacon when you don’t need it.

Turn callouts off if you want quiet.

Use VoiceVista in a way—

that supports your attention—

not competes with it.

you’ve learned the foundations of VoiceVista.

Exploration.

Awareness.

Destinations.

Markers.

And everyday usage.

The best way to continue learning—

is simple.

Go outside.

Put on your headphones.

And explore.
