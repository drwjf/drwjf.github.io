Welcome back.

Today—

we’re talking about one of VoiceVista’s most recognizable features.

The beacon.

If there is one feature people remember after trying VoiceVista—

it’s usually this one.

The easiest way to think about the beacon is this.

It is a lighthouse for your ears.

Choose a destination.

Start a beacon.

VoiceVista begins playing a sound—

from the direction of that destination.

But there is something important to understand.

The beacon does not tell you where to walk.

It does not tell you when to cross.

It does not choose your route.

Instead—

it answers one simple question.

Where is my destination—

relative to me?

Imagine your destination is northeast.

As you walk—

the beacon remains anchored in that direction.

If you turn—

the sound moves.

If you change course—

the sound changes.

Over time—

this helps you stay connected to your destination—

without constantly checking a screen.

Now let’s talk about the ring.

When you point more directly toward your destination—

you may hear a higher pitched confirmation sound.

Think of this as refinement.

Not instruction.

The ring is not saying—

walk this way.

It is simply helping you confirm direction.

Try this exercise.

Choose a nearby destination.

Set a beacon.

Stand still.

Slowly rotate.

Listen for changes.

Now begin walking.

Notice how the relationship between you—

and the destination—

changes over time.

One question new users often ask is—

why does the beacon sometimes become quieter?

VoiceVista needs confidence about which direction you are facing.

If the phone is in your pocket—

and you stop moving—

that information becomes less certain.

So the beacon becomes quieter.

Usually—

the solution is simple.

Start walking again.

Or—

hold the phone flat.

Screen toward the sky.

Top of the phone facing forward.

Another useful technique—

you do not need to leave the beacon active all the time.

Many experienced users mute the rhythmic beacon—

during familiar sections of a route.

Then turn it back on—

when they want more precise awareness.

You can place a beacon on many kinds of locations.

Businesses.

Addresses.

Intersections.

Places.

And saved markers.

Here’s a fun experiment.

Set a beacon on your home.

Walk around the block.

Notice how the sound shifts—

as your position changes.

You may discover new ways—

of understanding your neighborhood.

Remember—

the beacon isn’t trying to lead you.

It’s helping you stay connected—

to where you want to go.

Next time—

we’ll learn how markers and routes—

can turn individual journeys—

into something you can save—

and reuse.

Thanks for listening.
